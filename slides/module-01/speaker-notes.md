# Module 1 Speaker Notes

Internal budget: 12-minute onboarding plus 25-minute core. Rehearse the six-step
read-only Lab 3; content validation does not verify fresh learner access.

## Slide 1 - Get your lab account

**2 minutes, onboarding.** Smoke-test the actual access route. Never project TAPs.

## Slide 2 - Use the approved access process

**3 minutes.** Explain minimal collection and submit once. Confirm workflow success.

## Slide 3 - Check the lab-access email

**4 minutes.** Check username, actual TAP lifetime and route privately. Support late
delivery without duplicate accounts.

## Slide 4 - Sign in using the approved method

**1 minute.** Confirm the lab account. No CA/MFA/device-policy bypass.

## Slide 5 - Confirm readiness without extra privileges

**2 minutes.** AI Reader is read-only inventory access, not maker or broad admin.
No deployment, chat or knowledge test. Move access failures to a helper.

## Slide 6 - See It. Control It. Protect It.

**1 minute.** Start with useful work and purpose, people, boundaries and evidence.

## Slide 7 - What does an agent do?

**3 minutes.** Read the employee's laptop-help/ticket request. Walk request,
information and action. Explain knowledge versus tools, then the need for
permissions. A conversational interface can also use tools; do not claim all
chatbots only answer or that all agents can write. Ask what makes ticket creation
different from describing it. No attack vocabulary, extra portal or build.
This is an original illustration, not Relay's runtime.

## Slide 8 - Why does agent access matter?

**1 minute.** Keep the same job. Contrast guide/ticket access with unrelated
payroll/account-management access. Define least privilege and identify who
approves the boundary. Other jobs may legitimately need other access.
Successful sign-in does not establish safe behavior. Identity misuse and
misleading content are later security explanations, not today's object/attack
lesson. No configuration. Shared course screenshots remain reference-only.

## Slide 9 - How do we know what an agent did?

**3 minutes.** Start with the claimed reply and ask what proves the ticket exists.
Then ask which recorded calls show the steps and what shows reliability across
requests. Introduce evaluation, tracing and monitoring after those examples.
State collection gaps and separate role/integration requirements. Traces do not
reveal internal chain-of-thought. Foundry grounds the concepts, not automatic
coverage or a new setup lab. Define a registry as a list of agents before the
review; listing is not task success. Ask the same accountable owner to arrange
evidence from the technical team, rather than introducing an unexplained new role.

## Slide 10 - One job. Four Microsoft services.

**1 minute.** Agent 365 inventory, Entra identity/access, Purview data/compliance
and Defender investigation have distinct coverage and roles. No four-portal demo.

## Slide 11 - Review one prepared agent

**3 minutes.** Launch six-step Lab 3; continue within slide 12's budget. Find the
existing Project Beacon Relay Agent through Registry > Total agents > search
Project Beacon Relay > Enter. Review owner, reach, risk, Activity and discuss.
Same selected record throughout. Security > Risk details must show High /
The agent is compromised. Staged training, not a live attack. Never use the
faulty Agents at risk count filter. Missing risk is unresolved, not complete.

## Slide 12 - Who owns it and what can it reach?

**5 minutes.** Return here after Lab 3. Agent 365 is the registry (discovery and
inventory); Entra provides identity and access controls. Walk the five control
areas and the registry field that is a clue for each: identity (Details: Entra agent
ID), accountability (Details: Owner, Created by), permissions (Permissions tab),
sign-in rules (Security tab risk) and network (not shown). Created by is not the
current owner, and sponsor, technical manager and approver differ. A registry field
is a clue, not proof of approval, expiry, policy results or inspected routing.
Report missing metadata and name who would confirm it. Coverage depends on the
agent and flow; configuration belongs in Modules 3-5. No new helpdesk deployment,
maker role changes or student writes.

## Slide 13 - What data can it touch?

**1.5 minutes.** Purview applies existing labels, DLP and audit to supported agent
activity. Audit records; DLP can block. The Security tab lists reported Purview
protections, which are not proof of a tested control. Blank fields mean unknown, not
no access. Workload coverage and licensing vary. Module 5 tests data controls.

## Slide 14 - What if the agent gets it wrong?

**1 minute.** The support agent from slides 7-9, not Relay: a laptop ticket lands in
the wrong queue. Walk Spot it > Find who > Investigate > Contain > Check it worked >
Fix the gap. Two causes are possible, misused access or misleading content, and a
valid sign-in cannot tell them apart. Activity is inventory usage, not full traces or
security/audit proof. Entra, Purview and Defender follow-up needs separate roles.
An event, alert and incident differ; Defender integration is not automatic and the
ISOC preview is not required. Containment is an authorized-team task in Module 7.

## Slide 15 - Is the agent worth it?

**1.5 minutes.** Ask whether the support agent solved the problem, saved effort and
justified its cost, compared with a baseline. Registry Activity shows usage, not
value. Many chats can still mean many failed requests. Module 8 decides adoption.

## Slide 16 - What can you now say about an agent?

**3 minutes.** Recap management, governance, security and business value as four
questions. Then ask orally for an observed fact from the Relay record and a gap it
could not fill, plus the accountable next check. No worksheet, copying or
submission. Missing access uses the instructor live view or remains an explicit
unresolved issue.

## Slide 17 - Establish normal before investigating abnormal

**1 minute.** Carry the method into Module 2. Using/testing and any optional maker
build belong there; current mail runtime/content remains unchanged. Do not imply a
replacement sample or optional path is deployed. Fresh learner access and timing
still need independent rehearsal.
